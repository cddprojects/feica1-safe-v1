# Canada job sites: what they say, and what a safer site should say

Research date: 27 October 2026 (Asia/Kuala Lumpur). This note is for an independent Canada career publication in the same family as WorkNest SG. It is not a job board, and it should not read like one.

## The market, in one paragraph

Statistics Canada counted **510,200 job vacancies** in the second quarter of 2026. The vacancy rate held at **2.8%**. There were **3.0 unemployed people for every vacancy**. In September 2026 the unemployment rate was **6.5%**, employment fell by 68,000, and the employment rate was 60.6%. Boards still open with “your next job starts here” because they are search tools. The numbers say the market is steady and uneven, not wide open.

Sources: [Job vacancies, second quarter 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260915/dq260915a-eng.htm) (released 15 September 2026) and [Labour Force Survey, September 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/261009/dq261009a-eng.htm) (released 9 October 2026).

### What Job Bank calls “in demand”

[Job Bank’s in-demand sectors page](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/career-planning/resources/in-demand-sectors) ranks sectors by how often jobs were advertised over three years, not by the latest vacancy change. Its own line is: “Discover which sectors and occupations are in high demand and make education and training choices that are right for you.”

Top advertised occupations it lists:

- Health care and social assistance: home support workers and caregivers (NOC 44101), nurse aides and orderlies (NOC 33102), social and community service workers (NOC 42201). Job Bank says these outlooks are “very positive.”
- Construction: trades helpers and labourers, carpenters, construction managers. The page ties demand to retirements and new projects, and mentions 300 designated trades.
- Transportation and warehousing: transport truck drivers, automotive service technicians, public works equipment operators.
- Accommodation and food: cooks, food service supervisors, food counter attendants.
- Retail: retail sales supervisors, retail salespersons, retail and wholesale managers.
- Manufacturing: processing labourers, welders, food and beverage processing labourers.
- Green occupations (a separate list): civil engineers, plumbers, roofers.

Hold both facts in the same article. Care, trades and trucking are still the jobs employers keep advertising. The count of unfilled health jobs fell over the year to Q2 2026. “In demand” on Job Bank is not the same sentence as “vacancies are rising.”

## How Canadian sites actually talk

Checked 27 October 2026. Live job counts move every day. Treat them as examples of the sentence pattern, not as figures to reprint.

### Official boards lead with a search box and a job count

**Job Bank** ([jobbank.gc.ca](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/home)) is the national voice.

- Hero: “Your career starts here.”
- Next line is a count: “Search 84,126 job postings in Canada,” with keyword and location (“Current location” or “All of Canada”).
- Trending keywords shown on the home page: LMIA, Canada Summer Jobs, IT, Remote, Part time.
- Four tools, in this order: Job search (“Find a job”), Career planning (“Choose a career”), Labour market information (“Explore the market”), Hiring (“Fill a position”).
- Named audiences: young Canadians, Indigenous people, newcomers, foreign candidates, temporary foreign workers, persons with disabilities, veterans.
- Advice labels are plain: “Job search advice,” “Career planning advice,” “How to use Job Bank.”
- A static “by the numbers” block, date-modified 18 March 2025, says 105,000 job postings advertised (monthly average), 14.7 million views, 300,000 employers registered.
- Account pitch: free sign-up, job alerts, resume templates, and Job Bank Plus feedback within 30 days on whether an application was retained.

A browse page on the same site grouped live postings as Part time (6,075), Remote (683), and by province, with Ontario, Quebec and British Columbia the largest piles. Sales and service, then trades and transport, were the largest categories. Those counts are a live board, not the 510,200 vacancy survey.

**Canada.ca Jobs** ([canada.ca/en/services/jobs](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/jobs.html)) is a directory, not a pitch.

- “Over 80,000 jobs posted – how to search Job Bank.”
- Job Bank blurb: “Search jobs and apprenticeships from employers across Canada, get matched with a job.”
- Sibling links sit beside the search: Government of Canada jobs, youth and student employment, training, Employment Insurance, workplace standards, pensions, start a business, moving within Canada to work.

**WorkBC** ([workbc.ca](https://www.workbc.ca/)) is the provincial version of the same pattern.

- Hero: “Search 30,929 jobs in B.C.”
- Help line: “Looking for work? We are here to help.”
- Offer: free personalized support at a local WorkBC Centre, including resume help and local listings.
- Filters it teaches: location, job type, salary, industry, education, date posted.
- For short-term work it tells people to filter job type to part-time, casual, temporary and contract. It calls that the gig economy: short contracts, experience, flexibility, and several career changes over a working life. It does not promise income.

### Commercial boards use the same hero, with pay and remote as filters

**Indeed Canada** ([ca.indeed.com](https://ca.indeed.com/)) :

- “Your next job starts here.”
- Button: “Find jobs.”
- “Create an account or sign in to see your personalized job recommendations.”
- “Post your resume and find your next job on Indeed.”
- Filters job seekers actually see: Pay, Remote, Job type, Job Language, Date posted.

A Canadian job card, on Job Bank and on Indeed, is a short record:

- Job title
- Employer name
- City and province, written as “Maple (ON)” or “Thunder Bay, ON”
- Pay, usually hourly or annual, sometimes “to be negotiated”
- Work site: On site, Hybrid, or Remote
- Date posted
- Sometimes “Direct Apply,” “Green job,” or a program name such as Canada Summer Jobs

Indeed’s location rules tell employers to use the real work location. “Canada” or “Nationwide” is only for a role that can be done anywhere in the country. Remote and hybrid are separate from an office address. Readers already know that “work from home” in a search box also returns on-site jobs. A safer article should say that out loud.

### Rights pages are provincial, and they say so

There is no single Canadian employment-standards page that covers a part-time job in every city. Federal rules cover banks, airlines, telecoms, and interprovincial transport. Everyone else follows the province or territory where the work is done.

Pay disclosure is now part of how ads are written in the two largest job markets:

- British Columbia has required compensation information in public job postings since 1 November 2023.
- Ontario, from 1 January 2026, requires covered employers (generally 25 or more employees) to include expected compensation or a range in a publicly advertised posting. A range cannot span more than $50,000 a year. Postings over $200,000 a year are exempt. The posting must also say whether the employer uses artificial intelligence to screen applicants, and whether a vacancy exists. Canadian experience cannot be required in the posting or the application form. Someone who is interviewed must be told whether a hiring decision was made. The posted range is not a promise that the employer will offer that pay. Source: [Ontario ESA guide](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/requirements-related-publicly-advertised-job).
- Indeed Hiring Lab measured the Ontario effect: pay appeared on 49% of Ontario postings just before the rule, and on 71% by June 2026. Most of those were ranges, not a single figure. Source: [Indeed Hiring Lab, 23 July 2026](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/en-ca/2026/07/23/growth-in-canadian-salary-transparency-driven-by-two-provinces/).

Federal minimum wage for federally regulated employees is **$18.15 an hour from 1 April 2026**. If the provincial rate is higher, the higher rate applies. Source: [Canada.ca, pay and deductions](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/jobs/workplace/federal-labour-standards/pay-deductions.html).

In the Labour Force Survey, part-time means usually **under 30 hours a week**. That is the Canadian statistical definition. It is not Singapore’s “under 35 hours,” and it is not automatically the overtime threshold in a given province.

CPP and EI can apply to part-time employment. Do not print 2026 contribution rates from a secondary blog. Link readers to the Canada Revenue Agency.

A commercial 2026 employer guide says that, from 20 October 2026, federally regulated employers must pay part-time, casual and temporary staff the same wage as full-time staff doing the same work, and that a federal “right to disconnect” policy is law but not yet in force. That date was not confirmed on a Government of Canada page in this pass. Do not state it as fact until it is checked on the Canada Labour Code pages.

### Scam pages are short lists, not stories

The [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre job page](https://antifraudcentre.ca/scams-fraudes/job-emploi-eng.htm) is the voice to copy for a scam article. It names the schemes: car wrapping, counterfeit cheques, crypto “product boosting” tasks, financial agent / money mule, mystery shopper, and fake web-survey or online-store jobs. Warning signs, in its words:

- A webmail address instead of a company domain
- A job offer the person did not apply for
- A request to join a platform, download software, or do simple tasks for fast money
- Upfront deposits, “upgrade fees,” or “unlock payments”
- A balance on a screen that cannot be withdrawn
- A request to recruit other people
- A request to receive or forward money
- High pay for very little work

Protection lines: wait before answering, call the company on a number from its own website, never pay to get a job, do not download unknown apps, do not send ID to an unverified contact. Report to local police and to the Anti-Fraud Centre at **1-888-495-8501**. The CRTC uses the same number for recruitment texts that offer part-time remote work at high wages.

Pyramid selling is named as a criminal offence. That sentence can be quoted; it should not be softened into “be careful of recruiting friends.”

## What the safer site should say

WorkNest SG works because it is a publication. The footer says it does not recruit, place people, or publish vacancies. The Canada site should keep that line, and then talk the way Canadian boards talk about the topics readers search for.

### Hero

Boards say: “Your career starts here. Search 84,000 jobs.”

The safer site should say something closer to:

> What is changing in Canada’s job market, and what it means if you want a part-time job, work from home, or a side income.
>
> Vacancies held at about 510,200 in the second quarter of 2026. Unemployment was 6.5% in September. Articles here explain which sectors are still advertising, how to read a Canadian job ad, and how to spot a job scam. We do not post vacancies or place candidates.

Do not put a live job count in the hero. We do not have one. Do not use “Your career starts here” or “Your next job starts here.” Those lines belong to Job Bank and Indeed.

Primary button: “Read the latest articles,” not “Find jobs” and not “Apply.”

### Sections Canadian readers expect, rewritten as articles

1. **Sectors.** Health and social assistance, construction, trucking and warehousing, retail, food service, manufacturing. Say Job Bank’s three-year ad ranking and StatCan’s year-over-year vacancy change in the same piece.
2. **Roles and wages.** The ten occupations above, with offered wages labelled as advertised wages for vacant jobs.
3. **Work types people search.** Part-time, work from home, remote, online, freelance, gig. Define part-time as usually under 30 hours in the national survey. Say remote, hybrid and on-site are different, and that a “work from home” search returns all three.
4. **Side income.** Official sites rarely say “side income.” They say part-time, casual, contract, self-employment and gig. Readers still search “side income” and “side hustle.” Use the reader’s words in the headline and the official words in the body. Self-employment was up on the year and down in September. Pay is not guaranteed.
5. **How to read an ad.** City and province, hourly or annual pay, “to be negotiated,” on-site / hybrid / remote, date posted. In Ontario and B.C., say what a covered employer must disclose. Say the posted range is not an offer.
6. **How to read the statistics.** Vacancy, unemployment, offered wage and employee wage measure different things. The Q2 2026 story is a cooler market: 3 unemployed people per vacancy, and a job-finding rate below the pre-pandemic average.
7. **Pay and hours.** Minimum wage is provincial. Federal $18.15 only for federally regulated work, and only when it is higher than the local rate. Link ESDC and the reader’s province. Do not print one “Canadian minimum wage.”
8. **CPP, EI, and employee versus contractor.** Point to CRA. Say status follows the working relationship, not the word “freelance” on a contract. Do not give tax advice.
9. **Scams.** Use the Anti-Fraud Centre list almost as written. Phone number 1-888-495-8501 in the footer, the way WorkNest SG uses 1799. No helpline on the contact page if the Singapore cleanup removed it from there; keep it in the scam article and the footer.
10. **Audiences the official sites name.** Youth (unemployment 13%), newcomers, Indigenous readers, people with disabilities, veterans. A national page can point to Job Bank and the provincial service (WorkBC, Employment Ontario, and the Quebec and other provincial equivalents). It should not impersonate them.

### Words to use

part-time job, work from home, remote, hybrid, on site, online work, freelance, gig, casual, contract, side income, province, NOC, Job Bank, offered wage, vacancy, Employment Insurance, Canada Pension Plan, Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.

### Words and claims to leave out

- “Apply now,” “we are hiring,” “jobs near you,” “guaranteed,” “earn $X a day,” “no experience, high pay.”
- A grid of fake openings. Job Bank cards are real postings with an employer, a city, a wage and a job number. Invented cards would look like the thing the scam article warns against.
- “LMIA jobs” as a benefit. LMIA is an employer work-permit process and a Job Bank search keyword. Paying a stranger to arrange one belongs in the scam article, not the hero.
- One national rule for overtime, vacation, sick leave, or a “flexible work request.” Singapore has tripartite guidance. Canada does not have one equivalent sentence. Each rights article needs “check the rules where the work is done.”
- Quebec legal claims written only from an English federal page. CNESST and French-language workplace rules are separate. Say that, and do not invent the French text.
- Copied Job Bank or Indeed headlines. “Your career starts here” and “Your next job starts here” are their lines.

### Footer line

> This is an information site. It is independent and not affiliated with Job Bank, Statistics Canada, or any province. It does not recruit, place candidates, or publish vacancies. Articles are general information, not legal, tax, or employment advice. Confirm pay and workplace rules with the province or territory where the work is done, or with the federal labour program if the employer is federally regulated. Job scam reports: Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre, 1-888-495-8501.
