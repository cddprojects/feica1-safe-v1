FAQ

Common questions.

Does Job Compass list jobs?

No. We write guides to online, remote and flexible work and how hiring works. For current openings, try Job Bank’s remote filter and employers’ own careers pages.

Are you a recruitment agency?

No. We’re an independent publisher, not a licensed employment agency. We don’t screen, place or introduce candidates, and we never charge job seekers.

Why only online, remote and flexible work?

Because these jobs are often easier to fit around a main job, school or family. We stick to work you can do online or away from the employer’s office, with part-time or flexible hours. On-site jobs aren’t something we cover.

Why do people take a second job?

In August 2023, Canadians with more than one job most often said essentials (34.9 per cent), extra income (34.8 per cent) or work they cared about (14.5 per cent). Our guide goes through the numbers, and Which kind of work suits you? can point you to the right place to start.

Is remote work common in Canada?

Fairly. In May 2026, 11.4 per cent of employed Canadians worked only from home and 9.8 per cent had a hybrid week. Remote jobs on Job Bank are a much shorter list than all postings, so a job title plus the remote filter works best. Here is how to search.

What is the difference between remote and online?

Remote is about where you work. Online is about how the work is done or delivered. A job can be one, the other or both. See how the three compare.

Will I be paid a set amount?

Only if an employer offers you that amount in writing. Nothing on Job Compass is a job offer or a promise of earnings.

Which province’s rules apply if I work from home?

It depends on where you work, where the employer operates, the type of business and your employment relationship. Federally regulated workplaces follow federal labour standards. What a second paycheque changes has more, with official links.

Can I do this in addition to my current job?

Often, yes, as long as the hours work and your current employer allows it. In 2023, 89.9 per cent of Canadians with two jobs had at least one part-time job. Check your employment agreement for any conflict-of-interest rules. See our guides on flexible hours and pay for a second job.

Do you have French content for Quebec?

Not yet. These pages are in English. In Quebec, employment standards are handled by the CNESST, and workplace language rules come under the Charter of the French Language and the OQLF. Their websites have the latest.
