Second path

Why a second dream is online, remote or flexible

North Desk · Last reviewed October 2026

A second dream, on this desk, is work you want beside the life you already have. Canadians who already hold two jobs mostly name money. A smaller share names the work itself. The arrangement matters because a second commute often does not fit.

What people said

  • 34.9 per cent: to pay for essentials such as groceries or housing.
  • 34.8 per cent: to earn extra income.
  • 14.5 per cent: to do work they were passionate about.
  • The hours and the place decide whether a second job can sit beside the first.

A second job is already common

In 2023, 5.6 per cent of employed Canadians held more than one job at the same time, about 1.1 million people. Women were more likely to do so than men (6.4 per cent, compared with 4.8 per cent). In 2025, Statistics Canada’s annual count of multiple jobholders was 1,178,600. Holding two jobs can mean the main job does not cover the bills. It can also mean someone wants a field the main job does not give them.

Those August 2023 reasons come from a Labour Force Survey supplement. They cover workers aged 15 to 69 who already had more than one job. They are not a forecast of what a new search will pay.

Why the work has to be online, remote, or flexible

Most people who held two jobs in 2023 also held at least one part-time job: 89.9 per cent. A full second commute, on top of a full first job, is the arrangement that fails first. Remote work removes the trip. Flexible hours let the second block of time sit around a shift, a school run, or a class. Online work is the kind of task that can travel that way: support, writing, design, bookkeeping, tutoring, software.

Home is already a workplace for a minority of employed Canadians. In May 2026, 11.4 per cent worked only from home, and 9.8 per cent split the week between home and another place. That is the whole economy, not a promise that a posting with the word “remote” is waiting. It does show that working from home is a normal arrangement, not a slogan.

Match the reason to the search

If the reason is essentials or extra income, start with the hours you can actually give and the pay basis you need: an employee wage, with a pay stub, is easier to plan around than a contract that can go quiet. If the reason is the work itself, start from a skill you can already show, and read the CV guide. If the reason is school, care, or health, read flexible hours before you read a posting that says “full-time remote.”

Then look in a specific place. Where to look walks through Job Bank’s remote filter and hours filter. Which guide fits points you at the page that matches your reason. Nothing here is an offer of work or a forecast of earnings.
