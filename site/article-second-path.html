Second jobs

Why people take a second job

Job Compass · Last reviewed October 2026

A second job or additional source of income may help with household expenses, build experience in a field you care about or provide work that fits around existing commitments.

What people said

  • 34.9 per cent: to pay for essentials such as groceries or housing.
  • 34.8 per cent: to earn extra income.
  • 14.5 per cent: to do work they were passionate about.
  • The hours and the place decide whether a second job can sit beside the first.

A second job is already common

In 2023, 5.6 per cent of employed Canadians held more than one job at the same time, about 1.1 million people. Women were more likely to do so than men (6.4 per cent, compared with 4.8 per cent). In 2025, Statistics Canada’s annual count of multiple jobholders was 1,178,600. Holding two jobs can mean the main job does not cover the bills. It can also mean someone wants a field the main job does not give them.

Those August 2023 reasons come from a Labour Force Survey supplement. They cover workers aged 15 to 69 who already had more than one job. They are not a forecast of what a new search will pay.

Why online, remote or flexible work may help

Most people who held two jobs in 2023 also held at least one part-time job: 89.9 per cent. A second commute can make scheduling more difficult. Remote work removes that trip, while flexible hours may fit around another shift, school, family responsibilities or a class. Online work can include customer support, writing, design, bookkeeping, tutoring and software development.

Home is already a workplace for a minority of employed Canadians. In May 2026, 11.4 per cent worked only from home, and 9.8 per cent split the week between home and another place. That is the whole economy, not a promise that a posting with the word “remote” is waiting. It does show that working from home is a normal arrangement, not a slogan.

Match the reason to the search

If your goal is to cover essentials or add income, start with the hours you can realistically work and the type of pay you need. Regular employee earnings may be more predictable than contract income that varies from month to month. If you want to build experience in a field, begin with skills you can already demonstrate and read the resume guide. If you need work to fit around school, care or health needs, read about flexible hours before applying to a full-time remote position.

Then search in a specific place. Where to look explains Job Bank’s remote and hours filters. Find the right guide directs you to information that matches your reason. Nothing here is an offer of work or a forecast of earnings.
