How the work is delivered

Online work in Canada

Online work is done or delivered over the internet. That doesn’t always mean you can work from home, or from any province.

At a glance

Typical tasks
Support, writing, design, bookkeeping, tutoring, moderation, software.
Status
Employee or independent contractor. What a posting calls the job doesn’t decide your legal status.
Pay
Hourly, salary or per project. A number in a posting isn’t an offer yet.
Additional work
Part-time support, writing, bookkeeping, design, tutoring or coding.

Last reviewed October 2026. This page is a guide, not a job posting.

Online work is not necessarily remote work

Online work can be done from the employer’s office or from somewhere else. Remote work can also include things like scheduled phone shifts that few people would call “online.” On Job Compass, online work means tasks done or delivered over the internet. The posting should say where you would actually do it.

Employee or contractor

Some online jobs are regular employment: you get a schedule, a manager and a pay stub, and Canada Pension Plan and Employment Insurance deductions may apply. Others are contract work, where you send invoices, carry the financial risk and handle your own taxes. The Canada Revenue Agency looks at control, tools, financial risk and chance of profit, so calling a job “freelance” doesn’t settle it. If you’re unsure, the CRA can help. This page is general information, not tax advice.

Online work beside a main job

Many people add online work to a full-time job by doing something they already know for a few hours a week: customer service, bookkeeping, writing, design, tutoring, moderating or coding. Think about the skills you can already show and whether the hours really fit your week. Our resume guide shows how to present that experience.

Software development is one occupation where online delivery is common. In the second quarter of 2026, Statistics Canada counted 3,545 vacancies for software developers and programmers, up about 35 per cent year over year, with an average offered wage of $43.65 an hour. That is the wage employers advertised for open positions in the occupation. It isn’t a typical remote-work wage, and it isn’t an offer to you.

A good posting names the organization, describes the work and explains how you would be paid. Ready to look? Start with Job Bank’s remote filter and the employer’s own careers page.
