How the work is delivered

Online work in Canada

Online work is completed or delivered over the internet. Work location is a separate condition and may be limited to a province.

At a glance

Typical tasks
Support, writing, design, bookkeeping, tutoring, moderation, software.
Status
Employee or independent contractor. The label in a posting does not determine legal status.
Pay
Hourly, salaried or project-based. An advertised figure is not a written offer.
Location
May be remote, hybrid or on-site; “online” alone does not specify where the work is done.

Last reviewed October 2026.

Online work is not necessarily remote work

Online work can be done from an employer’s workplace or somewhere else. Remote work can also include scheduled phone duties that are not usually described as online work. On this site, online work means tasks completed or delivered over the internet. Work location is a separate detail in the posting.

Employment and independent contract work

Some online roles are employment, with scheduled hours, supervision and payroll deductions for CPP or QPP and EI where applicable. Others are contract work involving invoices, business risk and self-employed tax obligations. The Canada Revenue Agency considers factors such as control, tools, financial risk and opportunity for profit. The word “freelance” does not determine employment status.

Online work alongside a main job

Online work alongside a main job often uses existing skills such as customer service, bookkeeping, writing, design, tutoring, moderation or software development. Prior experience in the occupation and hours that fit existing commitments are practical filters when comparing postings. The résumé guide covers relevant experience.

Occupation, work location, hours, employer and pay arrangement are separate search criteria. The guide to searching for remote work covers Job Bank filters and employer verification.
