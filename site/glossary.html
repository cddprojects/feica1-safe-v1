Glossary

Terms used in job postings

Plain-language definitions of terms found in Canadian job postings.

Remote
Work is completed away from the employer’s workplace. The position may still be limited to a province or time zone.
Hybrid
Part of the work is remote and part is at a set location. It is not fully remote.
On-site
Work completed at a location set by the employer, with no remote option.
Online work
Work done or delivered over the internet. The term alone does not specify location or hours.
Flexible work
Work that offers some choice over hours, schedule or workload.
Part-time
Usually fewer than 30 hours per week at the main or only job in Statistics Canada’s Labour Force Survey. A separate second job may involve fewer hours without changing how the main job is classified.
Second job
A job held at the same time as another job.
Additional work
Paid work taken on alongside another job, studies, caregiving or other commitments.
Job Bank
The Government of Canada’s job search website. Its remote filter helps when location matters.
NOC
National Occupational Classification. A code for an occupation, used on Job Bank and in labour statistics.
Vacancy
A position an employer is trying to fill. Statistics Canada counts vacancies through a survey, so its numbers differ from the postings on a website.
Offered wage
The wage advertised for an open position. It is neither the average pay of current workers nor a written offer.
CPP
Canada Pension Plan. A public pension plan funded through eligible employment contributions outside Quebec; Quebec uses QPP.
EI
Employment Insurance. A federal program that provides temporary benefits to eligible workers; premiums are generally deducted from insurable employment.
Employee
A worker in an employment relationship. Pay normally runs through payroll, with statutory deductions where applicable.
Independent contractor
Someone in business for themselves. The CRA considers control, tools, financial risk and opportunity for profit rather than relying only on the wording of a contract.
Freelance
An informal label for independent contract work. The label does not by itself determine employment or tax status.
LMIA
Labour Market Impact Assessment. An assessment that some employers need before hiring a temporary foreign worker. The employer, not the worker, applies for it.
TD1
A federal, provincial or territorial form employees use so an employer can calculate income-tax deductions.
Gig
A short contract, one-off assignment or task, often arranged through an app or platform. Hours and income can vary, and some provinces have specific rules for platform workers.
