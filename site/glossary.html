Glossary

Terms used in job postings

Plain-language definitions for online, remote and flexible work in Canada. For legal rights or obligations, check the official sources linked from our articles.

Remote
The work is not performed at the employer’s office. It can still require you to live in a named province or time zone.
Hybrid
Some of the work is remote and some is at a stated place. It is not fully remote.
On-site
You attend a workplace. North Desk does not cover these roles.
Online work
Work that is completed or delivered over the internet. The term alone does not determine the location or hours.
Flexible work
Work that may offer some choice in hours, schedule or workload. North Desk covers it when the work is also online or remote.
Part-time
In the Labour Force Survey, usually under 30 hours a week at the main or only job. A second job can be shorter than that.
Second job
Work you hold at the same time as another job. In August 2023 the most common reasons were essentials, extra income, and work people were passionate about.
Additional work
A second job, freelance assignment or other paid work taken on alongside existing commitments.
Job Bank
The Government of Canada’s job-search website. Use its remote filter when work location matters.
NOC
National Occupational Classification. A code for an occupation, used on Job Bank and in labour statistics.
Vacancy
A position an employer is seeking to fill. Statistics Canada measures vacancies through a survey; its count is not the same as the number of job postings on a website.
Offered wage
The wage advertised for a vacant position. It is not the average wage of people already employed, and it is not a personal offer.
CPP
Canada Pension Plan. Contributions can apply to employment, including part-time employment. See the Canada Revenue Agency for current rates.
EI
Employment Insurance. Premiums and benefits have their own rules. A contract that calls you freelance does not automatically decide them.
Independent contractor
A worker who is in business for themselves. CRA looks at control, tools, risk and chance of profit, not only the contract’s title.
LMIA
A Labour Market Impact Assessment. It is a process employers may need to use when hiring certain temporary foreign workers. It is not a job or a document a job seeker purchases.
TD1
The form where an employee claims personal tax credits with an employer. Claim those credits with one employer, not again at a second job.
Gig
A short contract, casual assignment or task arranged through a platform. Hours and income may vary. Platform workers in some provinces may be covered by specific rules.
