The market

What the remote job market looks like in Canada

Job Compass · Last reviewed October 2026

Working from home is now a normal part of the Canadian job market. Remote jobs are still a small slice of all postings, which is why the remote filter helps.

Remote jobs are a small slice of the market

Statistics Canada’s Job Vacancy and Wage Survey counted 510,200 vacancies in the second quarter of 2026. The vacancy rate was 2.8 per cent. There were 3.0 unemployed people for every vacancy. In September 2026 the Labour Force Survey put unemployment at 6.5 per cent. Those numbers cover everything from offices to shops, trucks, kitchens and clinics. They are the backdrop, not the size of the remote job market.

Work-location data is more useful here. In May 2026, 11.4 per cent of employed Canadians worked exclusively from home, and 9.8 per cent had a hybrid arrangement. Working exclusively from home was slightly less common than one year earlier. Anyone hunting for a second job narrows that group further by wanting fewer hours, online work or both.

Why the remote filter matters

When we checked Job Bank in October 2026, its home page showed about 84,000 postings, while about 700 were marked remote. The counts change daily, but they show that remote postings are a small share of the total. A job-title search with the remote filter is more precise than browsing every posting. Searching only for “work from home” can also return hybrid and on-site jobs. See how to search for remote work.

One occupation that can be done online

Software developers and programmers had 3,545 vacancies in that quarter, up about 35 per cent year over year, with an average offered hourly wage of $43.65. Offered wages are the amounts employers advertised for vacancies in that occupation. They do not represent pay for remote work generally. Pay varies by occupation and posting.

Part-time remote work

Part-time remote work is a narrower search than “any job,” so relevant postings may appear less often. A Job Bank alert can track new results. The national vacancy total does not measure an individual applicant’s prospects.
