A CV for online and remote recruitment

North Desk · Last reviewed October 2026

A remote recruiter cannot watch you work. The page has to show that the work arrived anyway.

Put these where they can be seen

  • A result you finished without someone standing next to you.
  • The tools you used, named plainly.
  • Proof you can write: a short summary is the sample.
  • Your time zone and the provinces you can work from.

What recruitment is trying to learn

For an office role, a CV can lean on presence. For online and remote roles, the open question is whether the work gets done when nobody can see the chair. Write one line per role that names the output: a queue you cleared, a report you shipped, a class you taught, a release you maintained. Hours you are willing to overlap with a team belong in the header, next to the city and province.

What to leave off

Do not invent a remote title you did not hold. Do not list a daily earnings target. Do not attach identification, a social insurance number, or banking details to an application. A real recruiter does not need those to decide whether to speak with you.

If the only “interview” is inside an app you have never heard of, stop and read the scam briefing. Recruitment can be remote. It still names the company.

A short test before you send it

Can a stranger tell what you delivered, on what tools, in which time zone, in under a minute? If the page is a list of duties copied from an old posting, rewrite the duties as outcomes. Then apply on the employer’s site or on Job Bank, not through a link in an unsolicited text.
