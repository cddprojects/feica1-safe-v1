What belongs on a résumé for online and remote work

Job Compass · Last reviewed October 2026

A résumé for remote or online work should make relevant results, communication experience and digital tools easy to identify.

Relevant résumé information

  • Measurable results from work completed with limited day-to-day supervision
  • Examples of work completed with limited supervision.
  • Relevant communication, collaboration and project tools.
  • City and province when location eligibility matters

What employers look for

Online and remote applications benefit from evidence of reliable work with limited supervision. Relevant examples include customer requests resolved, reports delivered, classes taught or software releases maintained. Communication and project tools belong with the work where they were used. Availability is normally better placed in an application or cover letter than at the top of a résumé.

Information not needed in an application

Applications should not include a social insurance number, identification or banking details. A city and province are normally sufficient for location eligibility. Contract postings may request a business name or portfolio; employment postings normally request a résumé and, in some cases, a cover letter.

Résumé review

One or two quantified examples for each relevant role are generally more informative than a copied duty list. The search guide covers where postings appear.
