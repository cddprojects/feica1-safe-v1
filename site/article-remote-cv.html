Your application

Tailor your resume for online and remote work

Job Compass · Last reviewed October 2026

For work completed at a distance, your resume should show that you can produce results, communicate clearly and work with limited supervision.

What to highlight

  • Results you achieved while working independently.
  • The tools you used, described plainly.
  • A concise professional summary.
  • Your location and any geographic work restrictions that matter.

What an employer wants to learn

For online and remote positions, employers want to know whether you can complete work reliably with limited supervision. For each relevant role, describe a result: customer requests resolved, reports completed and submitted, classes taught or software releases maintained. Name the communication and project tools you used. If your availability is relevant, include it in your application or cover letter rather than assuming it belongs in the resume header.

What to leave off

Do not invent a remote job title you did not hold or include an expected daily income. Do not attach identification, a social insurance number or banking details to an application. An employer does not need that information to decide whether to interview you. State your city and province, and mention any relevant availability in the application or cover letter.

A short test before you send it

Can a reader quickly understand what you achieved and which tools you used? If your resume is mainly a list of duties copied from an old posting, rewrite the most relevant points as results. Then apply through the employer’s website or Job Bank. The search steps are in where to look.
