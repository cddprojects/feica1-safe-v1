Work types

Remote, online or flexible: which one do you need?

North Desk · Last reviewed October 2026

These terms often appear together, but each one describes a different part of a working arrangement.

Keep them apart

  • Remote describes where the work is done.
  • Online describes how the work is completed or delivered.
  • Flexible describes the hours or workload.
  • A position can have one, two or all three features. The job posting should explain which ones apply.

Work location

Remote work is done away from the employer’s regular workplace. Hybrid work includes both remote and on-site work. If a posting says remote but requires three days in the office, the position is hybrid. Read our remote-work guide.

How the work is delivered

Online work is completed or delivered over the internet. It may be done remotely or at an employer’s workplace. If a job posting says only “online job,” ask where you would be required to work. Read our online-work guide.

Hours

Flexible work may allow some choice over your schedule or workload. Statistics Canada generally defines part-time work as fewer than 30 hours a week at the main job. A flexible arrangement can still be full-time, and part-time work does not necessarily pay well. Read our flexible-work guide.

Why the difference matters

If you are adding work beside a main job, you need to know the hours. If you want to avoid a commute, check the work-location requirements. If you want a particular kind of work, focus on the duties. A posting that uses all three terms without explaining them leaves important questions unanswered. Next, learn how to check the details or read why people take a second job.
