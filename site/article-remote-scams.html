Scams

Remote-job scams reported in Canada

North Desk · Last reviewed October 2026

The pitches aimed at people who want online or remote work are already catalogued. They start friendly and end with a payment you cannot reverse.

Stop if you see this

  • A job you did not apply for, especially by text or messaging app.
  • A fee, a deposit, or an “unlock” payment.
  • An app or a platform you must install before you know the employer.
  • A balance on a screen that you cannot withdraw.
  • A request to receive money and send it onward.

What the Anti-Fraud Centre describes

The Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre says many frauds target people who are looking for work. The versions that dress up as online, remote or flexible jobs include crypto “product boosting,” simple online tasks, web surveys, and fake e-commerce store jobs. A small first payment is part of the script. Then the platform asks for a deposit to reach a higher commission. The balance you can see is not money you can take out.

Other versions send a cheque and ask you to forward part of it, or recruit you as a “financial agent” so that transfers land in your account and leave as cryptocurrency. The Centre warns that you can be treated as a money mule. Mystery-shopper and car-wrapping notes work the same way: a cheque, a task that looks small, and a loss when the cheque fails.

Pyramid-style recruiting, where you are paid to bring in other people rather than for the work, is a criminal offence in Canada. The Centre says so directly.

Warning signs, in their words

  • A webmail address instead of a company domain.
  • An offer you did not apply for.
  • A request to join a platform, download software, or do simple tasks for fast money.
  • Upfront deposits, upgrade fees, or unlock payments.
  • High pay for very little work.

What to do

Wait. Find the organisation’s phone number on its own website and call that. Do not send identification to an unverified contact. Report to local police and to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501, or through the job fraud page. The CRTC points to the same number for recruitment texts offering part-time remote work at high wages.

If you want a private pass over a message before you reply, use the scam check. It stays in your browser.
