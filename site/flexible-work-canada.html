Hours

Flexible online and remote work

Flexible work may offer some choice in your hours, workload or deadlines. Job Compass focuses on flexible arrangements that are also online or remote.

At a glance

Part-time
Usually under 30 hours a week, in Statistics Canada’s survey.
Shapes
Chosen hours, a shorter week, or a project with a deadline.
Not included
On-site casual shifts, even if the hours are short.
Pay
Depends on the role. Nothing here guarantees an amount.

Reviewed October 2026. This is not a job posting.

Why the hours matter in a second job

Flexible work can mean fewer hours, some choice over when you work or freedom from a workplace. We cover the location question in our remote-work guide. This guide focuses on hours and workload for online or remote work.

A second job beside a full-time one only works if the hours fit. In 2023, 89.9 per cent of multiple jobholders held at least one part-time job. People whose main job was already part-time were more than twice as likely to hold another job as people whose main job was full-time (9.7 per cent, compared with 4.7 per cent). Statistics Canada’s part-time line is usually under 30 hours a week at the main job. A second job can be much shorter than that. The line is statistical. It is not the overtime threshold in your province.

People also work fewer hours because of school, care for children or other family members, health needs or a preference for a shorter schedule. A job posting should state the weekly hours or deadlines, the time zone and whether you or the employer sets the schedule. Compare those details with our guide to reading a job posting with confidence.

Self-employment, which includes a lot of flexible online contract work, was up 51,000 people (+1.9 per cent) in the year to September 2026, and down 23,000 in that month alone. A flexible month can be a quiet month. Plan for that before you treat contract work as a wage.

Requesting a flexible arrangement

Federally regulated employees may have a formal right to request flexible work arrangements. Other processes depend on provincial or territorial law, the workplace and any collective agreement or employment contract. Make your request in writing, describe the schedule you are seeking and keep the employer’s response. Check the rules that apply to your workplace rather than relying on guidance written for another country.
