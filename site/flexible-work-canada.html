Hours

Flexible work, when it is also online or remote

Flexible means the hours or the load can move. On this desk it always sits inside online or remote work. A flexible Saturday on a shop floor is someone else’s beat.

At a glance

Part-time
Usually under 30 hours a week, in Statistics Canada’s survey.
Shapes
Chosen hours, a shorter week, or a project with a deadline.
Not included
On-site casual shifts, even if the hours are short.
Pay
Depends on the role. Nothing here guarantees an amount.

Reviewed October 2026. Not a vacancy.

Why the hours are the point of a second path

Readers use flexible for three different hopes: fewer hours, hours they pick, or freedom from a building. We keep the third one under remote. This guide is about hours and load, for work that is already online or remote.

A second job beside a full-time one only works if the hours fit. In 2023, 89.9 per cent of multiple jobholders held at least one part-time job. People whose main job was already part-time were more than twice as likely to hold another job as people whose main job was full-time (9.7 per cent, compared with 4.7 per cent). Statistics Canada’s part-time line is usually under 30 hours a week at the main job. A second job can be much shorter than that. The line is statistical. It is not the overtime threshold in your province.

People also work a shorter week because of school, care for children or other family, their own health, or a preference for fewer hours. Those reasons are why a flexible arrangement exists. The posting should still say the weekly hours or the deadline, the time zone, and whether the schedule is yours to set or theirs to roster. Compare that with how to read a remote ad.

Self-employment, which includes a lot of flexible online contract work, was up 51,000 people (+1.9 per cent) in the year to September 2026, and down 23,000 in that month alone. A flexible month can be a quiet month. Plan for that before you treat contract work as a wage.

There is no single national request form

If you already have an employer and want different hours, the process depends on the province, the contract, and whether the employer is federally regulated. Canada does not have one national flexible-work request with a fixed reply time. Ask in writing, describe the hours, and keep their answer. Do not assume a guideline you read for another country applies here.
