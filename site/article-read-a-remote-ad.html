How to read a remote job ad in Canada

North Desk · Last reviewed October 2026

A Canadian posting is a short record. Read it in order, and stop when a field is missing or invented.

Read in this order

  • Who is hiring, and can you find that name on a website you looked up yourself?
  • Where may the work be done: remote, hybrid, or a named province?
  • What is the pay: an amount, a range, or the phrase “to be negotiated”?
  • What are the hours, the time zone, and the employment status?
  • Is anyone asking you to pay, install an app, or move money?

The fields a real card has

Job Bank and Indeed cards carry a title, an employer, a place written as a city and province, pay by the hour or the year, a work-site label (on-site, hybrid or remote), and a date. Sometimes the wage says “to be negotiated.” That is an absence of a number, not a high number.

Remote ads add a constraint the office ads hide in the address: who is allowed to apply from where. “Remote — Canada” and “remote — must live in British Columbia” are different jobs. Indeed tells employers not to post a nationwide location unless people anywhere in the country can do the work.

Pay on the ad

British Columbia has required compensation information in public job postings since 1 November 2023. Ontario, from 1 January 2026, requires many employers with at least 25 employees to include the expected compensation or a range, to say whether they use artificial intelligence to screen applicants, and to say whether a vacancy exists. A posted range in Ontario cannot span more than $50,000 a year, and roles above $200,000 a year are exempt. The range is not a promise that you will be offered that pay. Canadian experience cannot be required in those Ontario postings or their application forms.

Indeed’s hiring lab measured the Ontario change: pay appeared on 49 per cent of Ontario postings just before the rule, and on 71 per cent by June 2026. Most of those were ranges.

What does not belong on the card

A fee to apply, a request to buy equipment from a link they send, a private messaging app as the only interview, or a balance you can see and cannot withdraw. Those are described on the scam briefing. You can also run the scam check before you answer.
