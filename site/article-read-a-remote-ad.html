How to read a remote job posting

Job Compass · Last reviewed October 2026

A posting is easier to assess when the employer, work location, pay, hours, employment relationship and schedule constraints are clear.

What to look for

  • Who is hiring, and can you find that employer on its own website?
  • Where is the work done: remote, hybrid or in a named province?
  • What does it pay: a set amount, a range or “to be negotiated”?
  • What are the hours and time zone, and does the posting describe employment or independent contract work?
  • Do the hours fit around your current job, school or family?

What to expect in a job posting

Most postings on major Canadian job boards show a job title, employer, location, pay, a work-location label and the date posted. Pay may be hourly or yearly. If the wage says “to be negotiated,” the posting does not provide an amount.

Remote postings normally state the permitted work location. “Remote, Canada” and “Remote, must live in British Columbia” describe different arrangements. Employer guidance on major job boards reserves a nationwide location for work that may be performed anywhere in Canada.

Pay in the posting

British Columbia’s pay transparency rules require expected pay or a pay range in covered publicly advertised job postings from 1 November 2023. For covered publicly advertised job postings in Ontario, rules in effect from 1 January 2026 require many employers with at least 25 employees to include expected compensation or a range, disclose whether they use artificial intelligence to screen applicants and say whether a vacancy exists. A posted range in Ontario cannot span more than $50,000 a year, and positions paying more than $200,000 a year are exempt. A posted range does not fix the amount in an offer of employment. Employers cannot require Canadian experience in those Ontario postings or related application forms.

Indeed’s hiring lab measured the Ontario change: pay appeared on 49 per cent of Ontario postings just before the rule, and on 71 per cent by June 2026. Most of those were ranges.

Hours, time zone and pay for a second job

For a second job, stated weekly hours and time zone usually matter more than a “flexible” label. A posted pay range describes the vacancy; the amount in a written offer may differ. Search steps are covered in How to search for remote work.
