How to read a remote job posting

Job Compass · Last reviewed October 2026

A few details tell you whether a job is worth your time. Look at who the employer is, then at the location, pay, hours and work arrangement.

What to look for

  • Who is hiring, and can you find that employer on its own website?
  • Where is the work done: remote, hybrid or in a named province?
  • What does it pay: a set amount, a range or “to be negotiated”?
  • What are the hours and time zone, and would you be an employee or a contractor?
  • Do the hours fit around your current job, school or family?

What to expect in a job posting

Most Job Bank and Indeed postings show a job title, employer, location, pay, a work-location label and the date posted. Pay may be hourly or yearly. If the wage says “to be negotiated,” the posting does not provide an amount.

Remote postings normally include the permitted work location. “Remote, Canada” and “Remote, must live in British Columbia” describe different arrangements. Indeed asks employers to use a nationwide location only when the job can be done from anywhere in the country.

Pay in the posting

British Columbia has required compensation information in covered public job postings since 1 November 2023. For covered publicly advertised job postings in Ontario, rules in effect from 1 January 2026 require many employers with at least 25 employees to include expected compensation or a range, disclose whether they use artificial intelligence to screen applicants and say whether a vacancy exists. A posted range in Ontario cannot span more than $50,000 a year, and positions paying more than $200,000 a year are exempt. A range does not guarantee the amount you will be offered. Employers cannot require Canadian experience in those Ontario postings or related application forms.

Indeed’s hiring lab measured the Ontario change: pay appeared on 49 per cent of Ontario postings just before the rule, and on 71 per cent by June 2026. Most of those were ranges.

Hours for a second job

For a second job, the weekly hours and time zone matter more than the word “flexible.” A pay range describes the job. It only becomes your pay once the employer puts that amount in an offer. Still deciding where to look? See our guide to finding remote work.
