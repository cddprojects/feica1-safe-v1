Why it matters

A second job can serve more than one purpose.

It may help with everyday costs, create room to build experience or offer hours that fit around the life you already have. A useful search starts with knowing what you want the work to change.

Start with your reason

There is no single reason to look for additional work.

Statistics Canada asked people who already held more than one job about their main reason in August 2023. Their answers show that income matters, but it is not the whole story.

Help with everyday costs

34.9 per cent said their main reason was paying for essentials such as groceries or housing.

Add income

34.8 per cent wanted income beyond what their main job provided.

Build experience

14.5 per cent wanted to do work they were passionate about. A second job can also be a practical way to develop experience in a field.

Find hours that fit

Part-time or flexible work may fit around a main job, studies, family responsibilities or health needs.

These figures describe people aged 15 to 69 who already held more than one job. They do not predict what a new job search will provide.

What these arrangements can change

Place, access and time are different needs.

Remote work can reduce or remove a commute, but a position may still require you to live in a particular province or work in a stated time zone.

Online work can make some tasks available from a computer, but online does not always mean remote or flexible.

Flexible work may offer a shorter schedule or some choice over hours. The posting should still explain who sets the schedule, how much work is available and how you will be paid.

The idea behind North Desk

Clear information before a job application.

Information about remote work is spread across job postings, Job Bank, Statistics Canada, the Canada Revenue Agency and provincial or territorial websites. It can be difficult to know which details matter to your situation.

North Desk brings that information together in plain language. Our guides help you choose a work arrangement, narrow a search, read a job posting, prepare a resume and understand basic questions about pay and employment status.

We provide independent guidance. We do not publish jobs, place candidates or promise a particular income.

A second dream

Practical needs and personal goals can exist together.

A “second dream” does not have to mean leaving your main job or finding a perfect role. It can be a second job that supports a household need, a freelance project that builds experience or a flexible role that creates a better fit with your schedule.

The goal is not to tell every reader that they need another job. It is to help people who are already considering one understand their options, limits and next steps.

What you can learn here

Move from a broad idea to a focused search.

  1. Decide whether location, hours, income or experience matters most.
  2. Understand the difference between remote, online and flexible work.
  3. Search by occupation and use filters that match your needs.
  4. Check the employer, work location, hours, pay and employment status.
  5. Use official sources when a legal, tax or employment rule affects you.
