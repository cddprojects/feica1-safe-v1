Additional work

Comparing options for additional work.

Hours, location, pay basis and employment status all affect whether additional work fits an existing schedule.

Four points to compare

What changes from one arrangement to another.

Available hours

Weekly hours, required availability and time zone need to fit existing commitments.

Pay basis

Employee pay is normally scheduled. Contract payment may be hourly, project-based or tied to milestones.

Work location

Remote, hybrid and on-site arrangements create different location and commuting requirements.

Employment status

Employees and independent contractors have different arrangements for deductions, expenses and workplace control.

What changes with each type

Where, how and when work is performed.

Remote work can reduce or eliminate commuting, though some postings still require residence in a stated province or territory or availability in a given time zone.

Online work is completed or delivered over the internet, but it is not necessarily remote or flexible.

Part-time work describes weekly hours. Flexible work describes choice over timing or workload and may still be full-time. The posting or agreement should identify who sets the schedule, how much work is available and how payment is calculated.

Employee and contract work

How the working relationship differs.

Employee roles normally provide scheduled pay and payroll deductions. Contract work may provide more control over workload, but payment, expenses and taxes are handled differently.

The label in a posting does not by itself determine employment status. The Canada Revenue Agency considers the actual working relationship.

Fit with existing commitments

Hours are only one part of the decision.

Required availability, commute or on-site days, expected income, equipment costs and possible conflicts with existing work can all affect whether an arrangement is practical.

A practical sequence

From priorities to a written offer.

  1. Priorities: hours, location, income or experience
  2. Arrangement: remote, online, part-time or flexible work
  3. Search: occupation plus relevant Job Bank filters
  4. Posting: employer, location, hours, pay and employment status
  5. Rules: the linked federal, provincial or territorial source
