Pay

What a second paycheque changes

North Desk · Last reviewed October 2026

A second job can add useful income, but it also adds a few details to check: the province tied to the work, the wage floor and how deductions are handled.

The short version

  • There is no single Canadian minimum wage for a laptop job. Check the federal rate and the province named on the posting.
  • Banks, airlines, telecoms and interprovincial transport follow the Canada Labour Code.
  • Job Bank says a remote posting follows the wage rules of the employer’s province, which can differ from yours.
  • A second employer still deducts tax and Canada Pension Plan. Personal tax credits on Form TD1 are not claimed twice.

There is no single Canadian minimum for a laptop job

Minimum wage is set province by province, with a separate federal rate for federally regulated employees. From 1 April 2026 the federal rate is $18.15 an hour. If the provincial general rate is higher, a federally regulated employer must meet the higher one. Alberta’s general rate is still $15. British Columbia’s is $18.25 from 1 June 2026. Ontario’s is $17.95 from 1 October 2026. Quebec’s is $16.60 from 1 May 2026. Confirm the current figure on the federal minimum wage database and on the province’s own site before you rely on a list, including ours.

Job Bank tells searchers that remote jobs follow the wage regulations of the province where the employer is located, and that those can differ from the rules where you live. Read the province on the card, then confirm it. This page is not a ruling on which statute applies to your contract.

What the ad must show, in two provinces

British Columbia has required pay information in public postings since 1 November 2023. Ontario’s rules from 1 January 2026 require many employers to show expected compensation or a range, disclose artificial-intelligence screening, and say whether a vacancy exists. Details and exceptions are in Read a remote job posting with confidence. The posted range is not yet your offer. Quebec has its own employment standards and French-language workplace rules through CNESST.

A second paycheque

If you already have an employer, a second employer still deducts income tax from what they pay you. The Canada Revenue Agency says each employer does this whether or not you have another job. Personal tax credit amounts on Form TD1 are claimed with one employer. If you already claimed them on the TD1 for your main job, you do not claim them again at the second job. Claiming them twice can leave you owing tax when both incomes are added up.

Canada Pension Plan works by employment, not by person, when the employers are different. Each employer deducts CPP until you reach the maximum for that employment. If the two jobs together deduct more than the annual maximum, the extra employee contribution is refunded or applied to tax owing when you file. The line for that overpayment is 44800. Quebec uses the Quebec Pension Plan, and the refund path is different. Rates change every year. Read the CRA pages rather than a number copied from a guide, including this one.

A contract that calls you freelance is not the test of whether you are an employee. The Canada Revenue Agency looks at control, tools, subcontracting, financial risk and the chance of profit. North Desk does not set that status. This is not tax advice.
