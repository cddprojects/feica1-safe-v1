Pay

Pay and deductions for additional work

Job Compass · Last reviewed October 2026

Additional work may involve minimum-wage rules, payroll deductions and different treatment for employees and independent contractors.

The short version

  • There is no single minimum wage for every remote job in Canada. It depends on the federal rate or the province or territory in the posting.
  • Banks, airlines, federally regulated telecommunications companies and interprovincial transportation businesses follow the Canada Labour Code.
  • The applicable employment standards can depend on the employer, worker, workplace and whether the business is federally regulated.
  • A second employer still deducts tax and Canada Pension Plan contributions. The CRA has current instructions for filling out Form TD1.

Minimum wage for remote work

Minimum wages change regularly and differ across federal, provincial and territorial jurisdictions. The federal minimum-wage database and the applicable government employment-standards page provide current rates.

The applicable employment standards can depend on the employer, worker, workplace and whether the business is federally regulated. The location in a remote posting is relevant, but it does not always settle jurisdiction by itself.

What job postings must show in B.C. and Ontario

British Columbia has required pay information in covered public job postings since 1 November 2023. Ontario’s rules from 1 January 2026 require many employers to show expected compensation or a range, disclose artificial-intelligence screening and say whether a vacancy exists. Details and exceptions are in How to read a remote job posting. A posted range is not a written offer.

Payroll deductions from a second job

A second employer still makes required payroll deductions. Multiple jobs can affect TD1 claims and may produce excess CPP or QPP contributions. The Canada Revenue Agency’s current instructions explain how to complete payroll forms and report contributions when filing a return.

A contract that uses the word “freelance” does not by itself establish self-employment. The Canada Revenue Agency considers control, tools, subcontracting, financial risk and opportunity for profit when assessing employment status.
