Pay

What a second paycheque changes

Job Compass · Last reviewed October 2026

A second job can add income, but a few things change: which province’s rules apply, the minimum wage and how deductions work.

The short version

  • There is no single minimum wage for every remote job in Canada. It depends on the federal rate or the province or territory in the posting.
  • Banks, airlines, federally regulated telecommunications companies and interprovincial transportation businesses follow the Canada Labour Code.
  • Job Bank says a remote posting follows the wage rules of the employer’s province, which may not be yours.
  • A second employer still deducts tax and Canada Pension Plan contributions. The CRA has current instructions for filling out Form TD1.

Minimum wage for remote work

Minimum wage is set province by province, with a separate federal rate for federally regulated employees. From 1 April 2026 the federal rate is $18.15 an hour. If the provincial general rate is higher, a federally regulated employer must meet the higher one. Alberta’s general rate is still $15. British Columbia’s is $18.25 from 1 June 2026. Ontario’s is $17.95 from 1 October 2026. Quebec’s is $16.60 from 1 May 2026. Rates change often, so double-check the current figure on the federal minimum wage database and your province’s website before relying on any list, including ours.

Job Bank says remote jobs follow the wage rules of the province where the employer is located, which can differ from where the worker lives. The province named in the posting is therefore an important detail. The applicable law can depend on the employment arrangement.

What job postings must show in B.C. and Ontario

British Columbia has required pay information in covered public job postings since 1 November 2023. Ontario’s rules from 1 January 2026 require many employers to show expected compensation or a range, disclose artificial-intelligence screening and say whether a vacancy exists. Details and exceptions are in How to read a remote job posting. A posted range is not a written offer. Quebec employment standards are administered by CNESST, while workplace language requirements are governed under the Charter of the French Language and overseen by the OQLF.

A second paycheque

If you already have an employer, a second employer still deducts income tax from your pay. If you have already claimed your personal tax credits on another TD1 and your total income will go past them, you generally enter zero on the second TD1 and tick the matching box. The CRA’s current instructions cover your exact situation.

Each employer works out CPP deductions on its own. If the two jobs together push you over the yearly maximum, the extra contribution can be refunded or applied to tax owing when you file (it goes on line 44800). Quebec uses the Quebec Pension Plan, which works a little differently. Rates change every year, so check the CRA for the latest.

A contract that uses the word “freelance” does not by itself establish self-employment. The Canada Revenue Agency considers control, tools, subcontracting, financial risk and opportunity for profit when assessing employment status.
