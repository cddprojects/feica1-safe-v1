Pay, province and a remote contract

North Desk · Last reviewed October 2026

A remote job still happens in a place. That place, and the kind of employer, decide which pay rules you open.

The short version

  • For most employers, workplace rules follow the province or territory where you do the work.
  • Banks, airlines, telecoms and interprovincial transport follow the Canada Labour Code.
  • The federal minimum wage is $18.15 an hour from 1 April 2026, and the provincial rate applies instead when it is higher.
  • A wage printed in an ad is information. It is not yet your offer.

There is no single Canadian minimum for a laptop job

Minimum wage is set province by province, with a separate federal rate for federally regulated employees. From 1 April 2026 the federal rate is $18.15 an hour. If your province’s general rate is higher, that higher rate is the one a federally regulated employer must meet. Everyone else’s floor is the provincial one. Alberta’s general rate is still $15. British Columbia’s is $18.25 from 1 June 2026. Ontario’s is $17.95 from 1 October 2026. Quebec’s is $16.60 from 1 May 2026. Confirm the current figure on the federal minimum wage database and on your province’s site before you rely on a list, including ours.

Some jobs have different floors: students, piece rates, and in British Columbia the engaged-time rate for ride-hail and delivery platform workers. If your “flexible online” offer is actually app-based delivery, you are in that rule set, not in a generic remote salary.

What the ad must show, in two provinces

British Columbia has required pay information in public postings since 1 November 2023. Ontario’s rules from 1 January 2026 require many employers to show expected compensation or a range, disclose artificial-intelligence screening, and say whether a vacancy exists. Details and exceptions are in How to read a remote job ad. Quebec has its own employment standards and French-language workplace rules through CNESST. Do not copy an Ontario sentence into a Quebec contract.

Employee or contractor

Canada Pension Plan contributions and Employment Insurance premiums can apply to part-time employment. They do not apply in the same way to a true independent contractor, and a contract that uses the word contractor is not the test. The Canada Revenue Agency looks at control, tools, subcontracting, financial risk and the chance of profit. Getting the label wrong is their problem and yours. Read CRA’s pages, or ask a tax adviser. North Desk does not set the status for you.
